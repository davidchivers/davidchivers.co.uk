Findings, ordered by importance

1. Production lineage stops at annual intermediate inputs

Inspected; upstream attribution unverified. The actual safe POI builder reads poi_annual_cbg_flows_<year>.parquet, an income-gap metadata Parquet, and an ACS lookup; it never reads weekly files. See code/analysis/build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:684 and :695. docs/data_construction/01_poi_processed_indices_and_audits.md:9 explicitly calls the initial audit staged-input-reproducible, not a raw rebuild.

The more precise historical production pointer is pipeline_poi_statewise.py, not merely the older pipeline_poi.py name. Log 18 identifies the former and replication_note.md at docs/data_construction/18_origin_cbg_geography_vintage_audit.md:35. Log 17 identifies config.py at docs/data_construction/17_visitor_worker_income_gap.md:31. These are descriptions of prior inspection, not source files inspected here. workstreams/tommy.md:11 attributes the pipeline to Tommy and lists the older POI/MSA/CBG run order, but contains no production commit, command, or execution receipt. No file with those pipeline names was found in the safe repository code/ inventory. A private or differently named implementation may exist.

Consequence: freeze the historical source/config/run identity before implementing an independent weekly reducer. Do not silently substitute a new download, the legacy monthly script, or today’s version of a similarly named pipeline. notes/07_poi_python_spec.md:33 already labels that monthly script legacy/partial and warns against running it.

2. Annual analysis weights are inherited, not independently reconciled

Inspected and synthetic tested; historical correctness unverified. build_visitor_worker_income_gap.py:124 maps total_annual_visits to matched_visits; lines 143-146 form matched_visits * 52 / weeks_observed. This is a naming/weighting convention in the downstream code, not proof that the upstream source field is matched visits rather than visitors or another expanded quantity. build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:545 copies this weight unchanged while line 550 independently records the sum of positive origin-map weights. There is no per-POI reconciliation between them in that builder.

The final audit uses the annual weight as supplied (run_within_venue_measurement_audit.py:102, :113). Synthetic inputs with weeks_observed=-1 and an arbitrary positive annual weight pass its weight screen because it does not inspect weeks or the origin total. This is outside that audit’s documented purpose, not proof of a defect in its moment algebra. Likewise, the earlier flag all_visit_weights_exactly_match compares total_origin_visit_weight, not annualized_matched_visits: compare_nhgis_full_poi.py:165, :192, :230 and check_nhgis_origin_cbg.py:169. Do not interpret that flag as independent validation of annualization.

The saved phase-1 audit reports a minimum of 26 weeks in every year and a maximum of 53 in 2018 (notes/08_poi_indices_phase1_audit.md:8). This is real prior evidence consistent with the intended 26-week filter, not proof that weeks were counted distinctly or assigned to years correctly. The instruction to restore MIN_WEEKS_OBSERVED=26 at workstreams/tommy.md:17 is weaker evidence than those output checks. A 53-week calendar is not itself a bug; it makes the calendar boundary and fixed-52 interpretation essential.

Synthetic reference: two venues with 10 observed flow units each, 26/52 observed weeks and diversity 0/.5 have an observed-flow mean of .25 but a 52-week-expanded mean of 1/6. Expansion changes the estimand and assumes the observed periods represent unobserved periods. Neither figure is empirical.

3. Existing-output reuse does not verify source identity or completeness

Inspected and synthetic tested; reproducibility bug. At build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:671, any existing output is reused without checking input/config/code hashes, schema or requested row coverage. Even a missing annual build-summary file does not stop reuse. --max-pois uses the same output filename as a full run (:671, :707), so an accidental smoke run in a production root can later be mistaken for a completed cache. The synthetic missing-summary cache test is an expected failure of the desired contract, not a fixed issue.

Recommended repair, not applied: require a matching build manifest and explicit complete/smoke status before cache reuse; otherwise refuse or rebuild in a new approved root. Preserve frozen historical outputs. Separate full-run row-count checks mitigate this risk when actually executed. Saved final counts in log 86 provide such historical evidence; this finding does not allege that the 2017-19 final files are truncated.

4. Full-build alignment check accepts trailing metadata

Inspected and synthetic tested; validation bug. build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:584 sets expected rows from the flow file; line 588 rejects only shorter metadata. Extra metadata rows with a matching prefix are silently discarded even when max_pois=None. The ID/order guard at line 621 correctly rejects permutations within the consumed prefix, and the synthetic tests confirm that protection.

Recommended repair, not applied: exact length equality for a full build, with a separately declared partial-run contract. The saved equal counts and IDs in log 20 are reassuring historical checks, not justification for accepting a future mismatched source pair. No prevalence in real inputs was measured.

5. Annual origin-map parsing is not fail-closed

Inspected and synthetic tested; input-validation gaps. At build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:449, orjson.loads silently retains the last value of a duplicate origin key. At lines 464-469, conversion to float followed only by <=0 accepts JSON strings "NaN" and "Infinity" as positive-cell inputs; the resulting total is nonfinite. Bare nonstandard JSON numeric constants are a different parser case and are not the demonstrated route.

The suite verifies the actual behavior and records three desired rejection contracts as expected failures. It supplies an independent strict parser reference that rejects duplicate keys, nonfinite values, booleans and negative weights while retaining positive weights. That is a proposed validation contract, not a replacement production parser or an instruction to alter the historical missing-value rule. Strict JSON syntax alone would not catch the quoted nonfinite strings. No malformed empirical input was inspected or found.

6. Income tie handling has a substantive measurement consequence

Inspected and synthetic tested; documented convention, not a newly found implementation mistake. build_harmonized_acs_2020_cbg.py:87 sorts income and CBG, uses unweighted CBG quantiles, and falls back to ordered ranks when bin edges are not unique. The NHGIS builder calls this exact helper at build_nhgis_acs_2020_cbg.py:304.

Ten invented CBGs with identical income are assigned ten different bins. A venue receiving equal flow from them has SES fractionalization .9 and normalized entropy 1, but continuous income variance zero. Thus these outputs describe home-area rank-group composition, not necessarily variation in income values. A tied-income CBG split can also depend on its identifier. Actual tie mass and empirical sensitivity have not been measured here.

An additional helper boundary is synthetic-tested: with fewer than ten valid CBGs in a CBSA, assign_msa_deciles uses labels 1..q (build_harmonized_acs_2020_cbg.py:146), while the POI code normalizes by log(10) and uses fixed low/middle/high cutpoints (build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:357, :376). In a two-CBG example both incomes become “low” bins. This is an unresolved small-CBSA interpretation contract, not evidence that such a CBSA occurs in the real lookups or that the primary national income measure is affected by that particular case.

7. Four-group coverage and final-audit scope need precise wording

Inspected and synthetic tested; interpretation/coverage limits. NHGIS allocates group counts before normalizing within the four retained groups (build_nhgis_acs_2020_cbg.py:191). POI shares average those normalized origin shares with origin-flow weights, then normalize again (build_harmonized_poi_indices.py:475, :238). This is not automatically equivalent to pooling full-population group masses across origins and then conditioning on retained groups. With equal visits to two otherwise homogeneous origins whose retained masses are 60% and 100%, the first convention gives F=.5 and the second F=.46875. This is a synthetic illustration, not an estimated omitted-mass correction. The exported shares alone cannot recover the latter.

The final audit checks the racial index identity on common mapped race-and-income support (run_within_venue_measurement_audit.py:128, :133). The suite confirms it rejects an inconsistent race index on that support but does not test the identity for a row excluded because income is missing. Therefore “all final-file rows were processed” must not become “every index on every excluded row was independently certified”. Missing income-bin shares still prevent an income within/between reconstruction (note 74, lines 88-92).
