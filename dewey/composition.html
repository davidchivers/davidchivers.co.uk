Why the income-diversity trend changes sign

19 October 2026 | Exposure, mixing and attitudes

Overview and rankingNew results and auditMeasures and approachData qualityEight literature reviewsSource library

Main finding

The apparent decline in average venue income diversity is an aggregate composition result, not a decline among the same observed venues. In the equal-venue calculation, the common-valid stable group becomes more diverse, but represents a smaller share of the recorded sample. The remaining group has substantially lower average diversity. Its growing share more than offsets the increase within the stable group.

This sharpens the warning in the first research report. It does not establish why the sample changed: actual business turnover, provider coverage and measurement/validity selection remain possible. Nor does it make the stable sample representative of all establishments.

The racial-diversity comparison does not reverse sign. Average racial diversity increases under both weighting schemes and on stable support. The composition contribution is negative but smaller than for income.

What was compared

The source is the previously verified national summary of the final 2017-2019 SafeGraph/Advan POI files obtained via Dewey, combined with ACS five-year home-CBG characteristics and the project’s NHGIS geography correction. No restricted data were reopened for this follow-up. The same 150 inherited mapped subcategories and common-valid race/income support are retained. This is new arithmetic on verified summaries, not a new regression or a replication of the original weekly construction.

The stable group contains the same 2,399,249 valid venue IDs in all three years with unchanged recorded area and category. The other group is the remainder of each year’s retained sample. It is not the same set of venues each year, and is not an identified set of entrants or closures. Stable IDs and harmonized metadata do not prove unchanged businesses or locations.

Income diversity is fractionalization across ten home-CBG income-rank groups. It measures diversity of visitors’ inferred residential backgrounds, not individual earnings. Race diversity is fractionalization across four retained origin-based race/ethnicity groups. Both are annual composition measures rather than observed simultaneous interactions. Numbers below are the indices multiplied by 100, not percentage changes or attitude effects.

Exact accounting

The overall mean is a weighted average of the two group means. We split its change into (i) the stable group’s mean change, weighted by its average share; (ii) the other group’s mean change, similarly weighted; and (iii) the change in their relative shares, multiplied by their average mean gap. Using endpoint averages treats both years symmetrically. The three terms sum exactly to the observed change, up to rounding.

These are contributions to the aggregate change, not causal coefficients. The stable group’s standalone equal-venue income change is +1.365 points; its contribution above is +1.107 because that change is multiplied by the group’s average share. The -1.817 share term is not an estimate of provider- coverage bias. Percentages of the net change are not reported: opposing components make those percentages misleading.

Why visit weighting differs

Stable venues account for 95.32% of annualized matched-visit weight in 2017 and 92.62% in 2019, a much smaller share change than in venue count. The other group matters much less when weighting by recorded visits. The resulting national income-diversity trend is positive under visit weighting, despite a negative share contribution.

Neither scheme is universally correct. Equal weighting describes the average recorded establishment; visit weighting describes composition associated with the retained annualized visit weights. Those weights are not known counts of independent people, and do not establish representativeness of all US visits. Stable-ID visit-weighted changes can also reflect redistribution of weight across those same venues.

Implications for the paper

Do not lead with a claim that income mixing fell from 2017 to 2019. Its direction depends on the establishment population and weighting rule. A fixed-ID comparison is an essential sensitivity, but not an automatic correction: it excludes nonpersistent and invalid observations by construction. Even its increase can reflect origins, ACS estimates or measurement rather than the same people choosing more diverse venues.

The immediate descriptive question remains whether comparisons between service types survive coherent within-establishment measurement and credible observation support. This accounting establishes a concrete reason to test those comparisons on fixed support, not a publishable behavioural mechanism by itself. The within-area venue-count association in note 89 survives the stable-ID check but remains descriptive; supply and preferences are not separately identified.

Priorities 3 and 4 remain conditional Green Book linkage and independent opportunity-list feasibility. Priorities 5-8 retain their earlier novelty, timing, cohort and input gates; this calculation supplies none of those missing data. Failed public-facing concentration screens, negative equal- county validation, stopped shock designs and null/contrary attitude results are unchanged. No public-facing or downstream coefficient was estimated.

Verification and reproducibility

Ten synthetic tests pass, including sign reversals, time reversal, missing years, changing stable support, invalid means/weights and small complements. All 12 empirical identities agree with a separate decimal calculation and the average of both sequential decompositions within 1e-10; the largest observed discrepancy is below 9e-17 in index units. The input hash matches the released summary. Its aggregation code confirms additive denominators on identical support. The first figure draft had clipped titles; it was archived and the final version passes text-boundary and visual checks.
