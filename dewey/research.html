Main result

The venue-count pattern is substantially weaker when comparisons are made within recorded areas. In 2019, the correlation between log recorded venue count and the share of diversity lying between venues is 0.568 after service category controls. It is 0.141 after adding area controls, and 0.246 after also allowing for recorded visit volume. The same pattern appears in 2017 and 2018. This is new empirical work on the existing data, not another literature assessment.

The result narrows the interpretation. The initial cross-area pattern is not strong evidence that more choice itself causes greater separation. The original separation-share and absolute between-venue associations remain positive; the conditional distinct-venue association is near zero after the same additive area/category controls in all three years and both samples. In 2019, the all-eligible adjusted correlations are 0.124670 for absolute between-venue diversity and 0.002518 for the distinct-venue contrast. This is a descriptive point-estimate qualification, not an equivalence result. Area controls remove shared local conditions, which can include substantive parts of a city-size mechanism as well as confounding. A change in correlation is not a percentage of the effect explained by geography.

What was run

The initial calculation uses the previously verified private area-by-subcategory-by-year moments constructed from the final NHGIS-corrected POI files. Those cells preserve the quantities needed for this particular comparison; they cannot replace raw venue identities for a continuity audit. The source is SafeGraph/Advan foot-traffic data obtained via Dewey, combined with year-matched ACS five-year home-CBG demographics and the project’s NHGIS geography correction. The census periods are 2013-17, 2014-18 and 2015-19.

The sample retains the inherited 150 mapped subcategories, valid race and income measures, at least two venues per cell and pooled racial diversity of at least 0.05. The 2019 primary comparison has 82,032 cells containing 3,247,365 venue-years across 933 recorded area codes. These are not 933 verified metropolitan areas: micropolitan and unresolved codes remain. Cells receive equal weight in the correlations; visits weight venues inside each cell. This is not an equally weighted POI regression.

For each cell, the racial measure is fractionalization: one minus the sum of squared shares across four retained race/ethnicity groups. The shares are inferred from visitors’ home areas, not observed personal race. Within-venue diversity averages the venue-specific indices using annualized matched visit weights. Pooled diversity first combines the visitor shares across venues. Their difference is the between-venue component. The separation share is that difference divided by pooled diversity.

Each correlation is computed between residual log count and a residual outcome after the listed additive controls. There is one calculation for each year, not a regression pooling years with a year dummy. The volume control is the log of summed annualized matched visits. We also retain a pooled-diversity sensitivity, with its accounting limitation below. There are no causal coefficients, standard errors, significance stars or selected p-values.

2019 controls Separation share Between-venue diversity Within-venue diversity
Subcategory 0.568 0.563 0.305
Recorded area and subcategory 0.141 0.125 -0.010
Area, subcategory and recorded volume 0.246 0.211 -0.071

All entries are partial correlations with log venue count on the same 2019 sample. They are not changes in diversity caused by one additional venue. The last row is not independent evidence that count reduces within-venue mixing: once pooled diversity is held fixed, the between and within components necessarily move in opposite directions. Volume is also an endogenous constructed variable, not an exogenous demand instrument.

Stability checks

The area/category-adjusted separation correlation is 0.141 when the minimum pooled-diversity threshold is 0.01, 0.141 at the primary 0.05 threshold, and 0.142 at 0.10 in 2019. Thus the finding is not driven by that denominator cutoff within the tested range.

A separate sample retains 72,328 identical area-category cells that qualify in every year. Changes from 2017 to 2019 have a count-separation partial correlation of 0.145 after area and category controls, or 0.179 with changes in volume added. This does not identify the effect of entry: recorded counts, coverage, demand and composition can change together. A balanced cell panel does not imply the same establishments are present within each cell.

Stable-venue audit

The separate, authorized pass through all three final files is complete. It finds 3,795,599 distinct venue IDs, of which 2,565,192 appear in all three years. Keeping common-valid race/income observations with an unchanged valid recorded area and category leaves 2,399,249 stable venue IDs each year. There are no detected area/category changes among the three-year IDs, but that may reflect harmonized metadata rather than independently verified unchanging businesses. An ID disappearing is not evidence of closure.

The 2019 count-separation correlation on stable venues is 0.579 with category controls, 0.142 with area and category controls, and 0.232 with volume added. Thus the attenuation is not eliminated by holding venue identities fixed. The stable-venue regression has 71,134 qualifying area-category cells; it is not the same cell sample as the 82,032-cell main comparison. Qualifying pooled-diversity thresholds can change from year to year even with fixed IDs. Stable IDs do not remove the comparison-object qualification: the 2019 adjusted distinct-venue correlation on that sample is 0.000925.

The unadjusted bin graph retains both sides of the earlier result: absolute within-venue diversity and between-venue separation can both increase across markets with more recorded venues. It is an equal-cell descriptive comparison, not evidence that adding venues raises both outcomes within a market.

The changing-sample decline is therefore not a general decline among the same venues. This makes sample composition a necessary part of any trend account. It does not prove whether differences arise from real entry/exit, provider coverage, demographic matching or other selection. Stable IDs are a selected sample; the changing and stable populations answer different questions. Changes also inherit annual census inputs and income-rank construction, so the stable-panel increase is not automatically a behavioural change.

Income diversity here is fractionalization across ten groups ranked by home-CBG income. It does not measure the size of monetary gaps or the personal income of visitors. The two plot panels use different vertical scales.

Nominal count versus effective count

Effective count is squared total visit weight divided by the sum of squared venue weights. It is smaller when a few venues dominate. On exactly the same 2019 cells and area/category controls, its separation correlation is 0.208, compared with 0.141 for nominal count. Among stable IDs the figures are 0.201 and 0.142. This is a concentration sensitivity, not an independent choice-set measure, customer sample size or correction for measurement noise.

The release also retains a distinct-venue pair contrast: between-venue diversity divided by one minus the sum of squared normalized venue weights. Its 2019 equal-cell mean rises from 0.0092 in the 2-4-venue bin to 0.0421 in the 100-plus bin (stable sample: 0.0085 to 0.0437). This removes the same-venue mass in the pairwise identity, not sampling noise or confounding. No iid finite-customer correction is justified by these annualized weights. The 20 October comparison extends this contrast to identical adjusted samples. All six area/category-adjusted correlations lie between -0.001651 and 0.002518, whereas category-only correlations remain positive. The contrast conditions independent weighted draws on different recorded venues; it is not a fraction of pooled diversity. The complete table retains the original separation share, between and within components.

Independent construction audit

The project is not unaudited: previous final-file and NHGIS reconstruction checks remain substantial. The new independent audit inspected the safe construction code and tested it on artificial inputs. It did not certify the original weekly-to-annual aggregation, which is upstream of that code.

Thirty-four tests were run and independently repeated: 29 pass their intended contracts; five deliberately reproduce unresolved validation failures. A successful test-run exit therefore does not mean production passed all 34 contracts. The demonstrated gaps are:

  1. Duplicate JSON origin keys can silently retain the last value.
  2. Quoted NaN origin weights are not rejected by the current parser.
  3. Quoted Infinity weights are likewise not rejected.
  4. A full build can ignore extra trailing metadata rows.
  5. An existing output can be reused without a matching complete-build and input-provenance manifest; a partial test run uses the same output name.

These are code weaknesses, not observations that malformed inputs occurred or that the released numerical results are wrong. Historical full-file counts and alignment checks provide additional protection, but do not replace fail-closed validation for future runs. Original production code and historical outputs remain unchanged. Forward repairs require explicit full-versus-test build manifests, exact full-build row alignment and duplicate/non-finite rejection, without silently changing historical missing-value conventions.

The complete independent audit gives the code references, artificial examples, passed contracts and replication-input list.

Two interpretation issues also matter. Income bins are ranks of home-CBG income, with a tie-breaking fallback: an artificial example with identical income values can still have diverse rank groups. Its prevalence in real lookups is unmeasured. The four-group racial measure conditions on the retained groups within each origin; omitted population mass cannot be recovered from the exported normalized shares alone. Neither diagnostic measures personal income, race or actual conversation.

What the inherited annual weights still require

The downstream code expands a supplied annual total by 52 divided by observed weeks. The original source field, distinct-week rule, 26-week filter and year-boundary convention must be traced to the actual production run. A saved initial audit reports up to 53 weeks in 2018; that is not automatically an error, but the calendar convention and fixed-52 interpretation need documenting. Agreement of annual origin-map totals is not an independent check of these expanded analysis weights.

That requires separately approved local access to the exact weekly members and intermediates. Raw records, credentials and unsanitized logs should stay out of this chat, website and shared Project. Other source-feasibility work

Independent opportunity data: libraries

A new outcome-blind audit verified the hashes and archive integrity of the already acquired IMLS Public Libraries Survey releases for 2017-2019. The outlet files contain 17,452, 17,478 and 17,496 records, with 17,267 outlet keys present in all three. Restricting to central/branch outlets not coded closed or temporarily closed and with usable numeric coordinates leaves 16,735, 16,801 and 16,812 candidate records respectively. This is a potential historical supply list, not a verified match to our mobility data.

Fiscal/reporting years need alignment with calendar-year visits. Hours/weeks and imputation flags need care: the 2017/18 outlet schemas do not contain the same hours/weeks flag fields as 2019. Missing flag columns do not establish zero imputation. Stable outlet keys do not establish unchanged addresses. The agency’s live landing-page fetch returned 403; this check used the hash-matched public archives already held, not a fresh download.

This opens a bounded alternative source check for the access question. It does not rescue the earlier library-investment event study: that design’s 38 treated systems remained below its locked minimum of 40. No mobility linkage, opportunity-coverage statistic or investment regression was run here.

IMLS source and the packaged annual public-use instructions identify the data and caveats.

Green Book

The source audit has moved beyond the previously blocked project website. NYPL provides verified CC0 structured data, and the Harvard replication deposit exposes CC0 aggregate schemas. Neither is yet the complete establishment-to-modern-site bridge we need.

There is an important version problem: the two NYPL 1947 derivatives contain 1,056 and 796 entries respectively, without a reconciled coverage crosswalk. The larger file does not include North Carolina. Coordinates and a licence do not establish a complete national sampling frame.

The strongest bounded lead is North Carolina’s official historical-resource inventory. Its current report distinguishes 63 extant, 226 demolished and 28 unknown resources, rather than keeping only survivors. But its master spreadsheet and exact reuse terms have not been obtained, and a 2024 site survey does not establish whether a firm/site was operating in 2017-2019. The report’s 317 resources and project page’s 327 businesses also require unit/version reconciliation; they are not necessarily ten missing firms.

Decision: narrow to a rights-cleared, outcome-blind historical-site pilot. First obtain the master inventory, its dictionary and licence, original edition identifiers, location uncertainty and dated transition evidence. Keep firm, site and district persistence separate; retain demolished and unmatched sites in the denominator. Unlisted businesses are not automatically discriminatory, and a listed business is not proof of integrated patronage. No modern POI linkage, proprietor extract, access-request message or regression was performed.

NYPL data, Harvard replication deposit, official NC report. The full feasibility audit records exact files, versions, access failures, schema checks and the proposed bounded pilot.

Verification and interpretation

A separate release reviewer found no blocking arithmetic, stable-selection or aggregation error in the inspected code and national results. The review reproduced the quoted changes and identified an imprecise figure caption, now corrected to annualized matched-visit weights, not independent raw visits. It did not inspect restricted rows, certify upstream construction or establish year-to-year income-bin comparability. In the stable equal-venue mean the ID weights are constant; in the stable visit-weighted mean they are not.

The new measurement code passes nine artificial-data tests, including explicit dummy-OLS comparisons, independent sparse least-squares residuals, stable-ID selection and weighted pairwise identities. The empirical cell pass reconciles the previous source/cell hashes, counts, weights and national moments. Its principal correlations were independently reconstructed with a sparse solver; the remaining threshold comparisons reuse the tested engine. The saved-cell pass took 17.97 seconds. The full-file pass, including source hashing and network-drive reads, took 1,234.46 seconds (20.57 minutes). It reproduced the prior common sample of 8,875,712 venue-years and national moments to numerical precision (largest difference 1.1e-16); input sizes/timestamps remained unchanged. Within/between identity error was below 2.5e-15. This reproduces final-file accounting, not the original weekly construction.

The cell results, continuity results and public-library readiness audit contain reviewed national aggregates and provenance, not venue-level records.

The protocol was frozen after the historical count result was known but before these new diagnostics. It is not a preregistration. All four control specifications, three years, denominator thresholds and balanced-cell changes remain in the reproducible output, including weak and contrary patterns.

No new attitudes, connectedness or worker-facing regression was run. The four failed public-facing concentration screens remain binding. Expected worker profiles are not observed employees. The negative equal-county validation, correction 70a’s Atlas-child weights, and note 69’s distinction between local deviations and category-FE coefficients remain part of the record.

Current paper assessment: the work is becoming a more disciplined description of visitor-origin composition. The count result alone is not yet a persuasive mechanism paper. The stable-venue results make a composition audit substantively important. The next decision depends on upstream provenance and a genuinely independent opportunity or historical-linkage contribution, not another search for significant outcomes.
