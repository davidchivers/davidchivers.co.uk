Question and closest predecessors

Question clarified 20 October: the primary Green Book branch is the legacy of historical locations in contemporary neighbourhoods, even where original buildings and businesses no longer exist. Their survival is not a requirement. Historical and current geography can explain an association, so continuing avoidance or discrimination is not identified by a map comparison. The dated literature assessment below remains as a record; its firm-continuity conditions are not universal gates on the area branch.

Source feasibility, updated 19 October: the new reproducible comparison finds 672 shared source-region keys between the 1,056- and 796-entry NYPL derivatives, with 384 and 124 unmatched entries respectively. Neither key set contains the other; 78 shared entries have coordinates over one kilometre apart. This is version disagreement, not a measured historical error rate. Neither source includes North Carolina entries. The NC historical-location route still needs its master inventory, exact rights, listing dates and usable coordinates. Its 2024 labels cannot establish 2017-2019 operation, but operation is only required for continuity claims. No historical-modern linkage or access request was made. The earlier literature assessment follows unchanged.

The defensible question is whether places that offered access to Black customers during segregation retain distinctive commercial functions or visitor composition today, and through which form of continuity. It is not whether historically integrated crowds remain integrated. A Green Book listing identifies an establishment advertised as accessible to Black customers; it neither counts a mixed crowd nor establishes that unlisted businesses excluded Black customers. Modern visitor-origin demographics would measure another object again: the composition of neighborhoods sending devices, not visitors’ observed race or their interactions. These distinctions determine the estimand before any matching exercise.

G1. Cook, Jones, Logan and Rose, QJE 2023, “The Evolution of Access to Public Accommodations in the United States.” County listing counts and business denominators measure historical access. The inspected August 2022 author manuscript uses White wartime casualties in difference-in-differences and instrumental-variable exercises, alongside a migration instrument. Its mechanism discussion allows demand and income changes as well as discrimination. This is evidence about changing accommodation supply, not observed interracial patronage. Publication metadata were verified separately; selected manuscript introduction, data and identification sections were inspected, not every robustness result. Published record; author manuscript.

G2. Jones, Logan, Rose and Cook, JUE 2024, “Black-Friendly businesses in cities during the Civil Rights Era.” This is the closest spatial and destruction predecessor. The publisher abstract and January 2024 appendix were inspected; complete main-text access was not obtained. Appendix A documents automated plus manual address geocoding. Appendix C already reports Callaway-Sant’Anna urban-renewal event studies, including comparisons of areas inside and outside project boundaries within the same tract. Listing coverage and historical address recovery require care. Treat this as a verified methodological overlap, not an independently audited causal result. Publisher; inspected appendix.

G3. Jones, Logan, Rose and Cook, “Competition and Consumer Discrimination,” NBER 33547, March 2025. Still verified as a working paper, not a journal publication. Selected model and empirical sections connect county consumer ratios to accommodation-supply ratios using wartime casualties and predicted Black migration, then examine competition and hotel prices. The competition proxy uses firms per consumer, not a conventional market-share concentration index. Its consumer-discrimination interpretation depends on the model and identifying assumptions; a modern persistence correlation would not independently establish those mechanisms. NBER record; author paper.

G4. Gil and Marion, JUE 2018, “Residential segregation, discrimination, and African-American theater entry during Jim Crow.” The publisher abstract and introduction describe an early-1950s theater census, reduced-form comparisons and an entry model relating segregation and discrimination to market profitability and entry costs. This is a relevant backward predecessor for endogenous commercial location, not a modern persistence study. Complete methods were not inspected, so the identifying restrictions cannot be certified here. Publisher.

Exact overlap and a remaining contribution

Simply placing historical listings on a contemporary map is an extension of the source geography, not yet an economics contribution. Likewise, estimating whether renewal removed listed businesses would overlap G2 rather than open a new field. The possible contribution is a transparent accounting of what persists: the same operating firm, a replacement business at the same site, or a commercial district that retains a function despite turnover. The detailed infrastructure comparison is in idea 7; the G2 digest is deliberately not repeated there.

Those three units imply different mechanisms. Continuing firms could transmit reputation, relationships or organizational practice. Site persistence could instead reflect building suitability, transport access or durable amenities. District persistence could reflect residential sorting, demand and commercial agglomeration. A paper that separates these possibilities would be more informative than one coefficient on distance to a listing. It would remain descriptive unless an independent source of variation distinguishes the mechanisms.

The strongest initial estimand is therefore a historical-site transition distribution, followed by modern composition conditional on explicit transition states. A useful question is whether a historically accessible commercial network disappears, changes function, or remains a locally important destination. Its value does not depend on a positive association with present-day racial mixing. A finding of extensive destruction, poor correspondence or no distinctive modern patronage could settle the feasibility question honestly.

Required versus currently established data

The project has saved modern annual exposure constructions, but their existence does not establish a validated historical crosswalk. Independent upstream construction verification remains a prerequisite to interpreting their demographic measures. No historical-to-modern match was performed in this review, and downloadable historical geography, coverage and reuse permissions have not been verified. A paper link or visible map is not evidence of a usable, licensed establishment panel.

A viable study would need edition-specific listing identifiers, original category and address fields, address uncertainty, an independently documented site history, and a dated modern establishment universe. Historical commercial denominators are needed to distinguish listing density from the density of all businesses. Modern origin shares and visit weights need a documented time window, suppression rules and geographical crosswalk. Historical owner identity, customer composition and continuing ownership must not be inferred from names or from Black access.

Every historical listing should enter the denominator before current survival is known. Outcomes must retain demolished sites, vacant parcels, noncommercial replacements, relocated businesses, ambiguous matches and unresolved addresses. Excluding those states would condition on survival, potentially removing precisely the effects of displacement or renewal that motivate the study. A current point-of-interest file cannot supply this denominator because it only represents observed contemporary destinations.

For surviving destinations, Black-origin visit share and diversity are distinct outcomes. A destination can preserve an important Black-serving function without having a highly diverse visitor pool. Conversely, a more diverse contemporary crowd can accompany displacement of the original clientele. Both outcomes should be interpreted relative to the surrounding residential population and a predeclared feasible destination menu, without treating either as a welfare ranking.

Design and threats

Begin with one historical edition and a small, geographically varied, outcome-blind sample of listings. Define conservative firm-continuity, site-continuity and district-continuity rules before seeing modern visitor composition. A name match alone is insufficient; a renamed business may continue while a familiar name may belong to an unrelated replacement. Address renumbering, relocated streets, parcel consolidation and geocoder snapping can create false precision. Report confidence classes instead of silently accepting every coordinate.

The comparison group should come from contemporaneous businesses of comparable category and local setting, not arbitrary modern venues. Otherwise the comparison confounds historical access with business age, location, travel demand and survival. Yet historical nonlisting is not a clean untreated state. The estimand must say “listed versus comparison businesses,” not “nondiscriminatory versus discriminatory firms,” unless another source independently establishes that status.

For present-day composition, show both an unconditional historical-site accounting and a clearly labeled survivor analysis. Matching on today’s neighborhood demographics answers a conditional question but may remove an important persistence pathway. It should not be advertised as the total historical effect. Conversely, omitting residential composition leaves open a simple demand explanation. Presenting both estimands is more honest than choosing the control set that makes the legacy coefficient persuasive.

Spatial proximity creates interference: nearby sites share customers, transport links and redevelopment. A ring around each listing may capture a district rather than an establishment effect, while overlapping rings double-count geography. Predefine mutually exclusive spatial units or explicit overlap weights, and evaluate uncertainty at the level where historical placement varies. Do not interpret hundreds of nearby venues as hundreds of independent historical treatments.

Minimal outcome-blind decision

The next authorized step, if separately approved, should be a bounded feasibility audit rather than a full national linkage. First specify the rights and geography questions using public documentation. Then define a small stratified sample without consulting modern racial outcomes, a comparison-business source, fixed match rules, and a finite review budget. This note authorizes none of those data actions.

The audit should produce a transition-state table, an uncertainty ledger and a missingness breakdown by historical city and business category. Advance only if it can distinguish site from firm continuity, retain disappearance in the denominator, identify a defensible comparison universe and establish modern measurement provenance. If the only reliable linkage is district-level, change the question explicitly. If only famous survivors can be matched, stop the national persistence claim; those cases may support historical illustration, not representative inference.

Recommendation and search boundary

This remains a plausible small, separate feasibility project, conditional on the preceding gates. It does not justify promotion to a causal attitudes paper or displacement of the core measurement work. The nontrivial opportunity is to distinguish lost access, durable places and surviving institutions, not to claim that the Green Book recorded integrated crowds.
