Main finding

The distinction between a typical venue and a typical recorded visit matters more for service rankings than restricting the data to stable venues. The income-background difference is mainly a within-area pattern; racial diversity also has an important across-area component. This is new descriptive evidence, not a regression identifying preferences, contact or attitude change.

The analysis uses the inherited 2017-2019 Advan/SafeGraph-derived POI files obtained through Dewey, with the project’s corrected origin-demographic construction. This step reuses their approved, locally retained continuity summaries; it does not reopen the original final files or reproduce the original weekly construction. Census home-area characteristics are proxies for visitor backgrounds, not observed personal incomes or racial identities.

The typical visit is not the typical venue

Equal venue weights give a small establishment the same influence as a large one. Annualized matched-visit weights give more influence to establishments with more recorded visit mass. Neither is automatically the correct population weight: they answer different questions and inherit the provider’s coverage and the project’s annualization rules.

The subsequent annualization audit held indices and samples fixed while undoing the documented expansion in the analysis weights. Its arithmetic checks passed, but its new result family failed the prospective disclosure gate and remains private. It therefore supplies no cleared finding about whether that expansion accounts for this weighting pattern; it does not replace or invalidate the published service comparison.

Is it simply geography?

A follow-up accounting exercise separates two sources of the weighting gap: giving busier establishments more weight inside the same recorded area and service, and giving different weights to areas. This follow-up was specified after the weighting difference was observed; it is not preregistered evidence of a mechanism. The same categories and all three years are retained.

Each entry is the equal-weighted mean across the same 136 categories, in index points, for stable venues. It is not the mean across all national visits. The two contributions add to the total before rounding. In the income case, most of the difference remains inside recorded areas; race has substantial contributions on both sides. The two ordered decompositions, rather than just their symmetric average, give the same qualitative distinction and are included in the aggregate release.

Economic interpretation: the recorded visits are disproportionately concentrated at venues with more diverse inferred income backgrounds, even within a recorded area and service category. This is compatible with larger catchments or services attracting a broader clientele. It is also compatible with observation intensity, suppressed small origin flows, venue selection or more precise estimated shares at busier places. The calculation does not choose between those explanations. It does not show that increasing venue size creates integration, nor that visitors meet or change their attitudes.

Measures and exact calculation

For venue j, fractionalization is F_j = 1 - sum_g p_jg^2. The racial measure uses non-Hispanic White, non-Hispanic Black, non-Hispanic Asian and Hispanic shares, conditional on those four groups, and is bounded by 0.75. The income measure uses ten national, unweighted CBG median-household-income rank groups and is bounded by 0.90. They are not ten equal-sized groups of people; the separate per-capita-income measure is not used for these bins. It measures diversity of assigned neighbourhood backgrounds, not the income distance between individual patrons. The final continuity summary lacks the income group-share vectors, so an income pooled/between decomposition is not computed.

For race, within-venue diversity averages F_j across establishments. Pooled local-service diversity instead applies F to the visit-weighted group shares pooled within each recorded area-service cell. Its excess over within-venue diversity is the between-venue accounting component. The category-level pooled number averages these local quantities; it is not racial diversity after pooling everyone nationally.

For the weighting accounting, within category/year let v_m and e_m be the visit-weighted and equal-venue means inside area m. Let q_m be its share of category visit mass and p_m its share of category venues. Then:

Visit mean - equal-venue mean
  = sum_m [(q_m+p_m)/2] (v_m-e_m)       [within-area weighting]
  + sum_m (q_m-p_m) [(v_m+e_m)/2]       [across-area weighting].

This exact identity averages the two possible orders of changing within-area and across-area weights. It is not a fixed-effects regression or a causal mediation decomposition. Recorded areas are not verified vintage-consistent MSAs: the inherited field contains metropolitan, micropolitan and unmatched codes. Different categories still cover different sets of areas.

Support and reproducibility

The common-cell analysis retains 71,492 area-category cells across 933 recorded area codes and 150 inherited categories. Stable IDs must remain common-valid with unchanged recorded area and category in all three years. Cells require at least two such IDs, yielding 2,376,783 stable venues each year. The earlier continuity analysis retained 2,399,249 IDs because it did not impose this two-stable-venues-per-cell rule. Neither number proves physical business continuity or national representativeness.

Eight sample/weight settings in each year give 24 settings. All 136 categories passing every original size/concentration screen form the fixed ranking universe; the other 14 labels remain visible. Fixed-market sensitivity uses the common-cell all-venue 2017 visit weights in every year and both samples. It holds geography weights fixed within a category, not its within-cell establishment weights or geography across categories.

Sixteen service-calculation tests, nine accounting tests and sixteen public-export tests pass on artificial inputs. Independent checks reproduced 8,568 ranks, all 147 comparisons and 16,119 numerical fields; the separate accounting review checked 2,295 artificial component calculations. An all-suppressed support edge case was fixed, then the empirical diagnostic was rerun in 9.57 seconds: all scientific outputs were exactly unchanged. This is a rerun of a small derived summary, not the original 20.57-minute final-file audit or an upstream replication.

Disclosure correction: independent review found that nested category means plus counts could reveal small complements by subtraction. The detailed local export was withdrawn into private storage before any publication. The public table therefore contains stable-sample levels, rank-only comparisons and broad component means, not nested category counts or individual components. This changes the release, not the scientific sample or results.

All categories and downloads include all 147 rank comparisons, all 24 rank settings and the six displayed broad accounting summaries. The full private calculation also retains all 18 sample/year/ dimension summaries; the public accounting excerpt deliberately uses stable support only. Source fingerprints, frozen protocols and the disclosure addendum accompany the sourcebook. No new attitude, worker, preference or causal coefficient has been estimated.

What follows

The results narrow the potential paper. Pooling is quantitatively important but does not radically reorder services. Weighting and support can alter interpretation more. A defensible contribution would establish whose exposure a service ranking represents, and whether the within-area weighting pattern survives independent construction and observation-process validation. Without that next check, this is a useful measurement result, not yet an identified account of integration or a publication-ready paper.

Dated research snapshot: new bounded measurement checks, independent safe-code audit and source feasibility. Not upstream weekly replication, causal identification or publication readiness. Historical results and failed gates remain in force.
