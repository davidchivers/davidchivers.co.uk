Question and bottom line

Subsequent empirical check, 19 October: the new results and graphs show a 2019 count-separation partial correlation of 0.568 with category controls, 0.141 with area/category controls and 0.246 with volume added. The area/category result is 0.142 on stable venue IDs. Effective-count and denominator sensitivities retain a positive association. This narrows the cross-area interpretation but does not establish preferences or supply effects. The literature assessment below is retained; its proposed noise correction has not been applied to the data.

Does having more establishments within a service enable people to sort into demographically distinct venues? Three propositions must be separated: counts correlate with a decomposition share; differentiated products support sorting; and entry causally increases segregation. The first does not establish the second or third. Even a perfectly measured descriptive gradient can reflect geography, demand, endogenous supply or the mechanics of subdividing a population.

The project’s historical count gradient is therefore a reason for a falsification and measurement audit, not evidence of a preference for segregation. A standalone paper would need information about meaningful product differences or a defensible source of supply variation, as well as a demonstration that its pattern exceeds count-related mechanical baselines.

Exact accounting and its limits

For a fixed market and service, let establishments have nonnegative weights w_j summing to one and normalized group shares p_j. Define p_bar=sum_j w_j p_j, pooled fractionalization P, average within-venue fractionalization W, and between-venue component B:

F(p) = 1 - sum_g p_g^2
P = F(p_bar)
W = sum_j w_j F(p_j)
B = P - W = sum_j w_j ||p_j-p_bar||^2
          = (1/2) sum_j sum_k w_j w_k ||p_j-p_k||^2

This identity requires the same support, demographic normalization and weights throughout. It measures variation in group composition across establishments, not interaction or attitudes. With K exhaustive groups, 0 <= B <= P <= 1-1/K. The between share B/P is undefined at P=0, not zero. Small P can make a ratio unstable even when its numerator is small.

With one venue, B=0 mechanically. More venues do not force positive B: all venues may have identical composition. Pure-group venues can give B/P=1, but fixed venue sizes, the number of venues and group totals constrain whether that allocation is feasible. Hence neither a universal monotone count relationship nor an unrestricted attainable maximum follows from venue count alone.

Splitting one venue into two records with identical compositions while preserving its total weight leaves P, W and B unchanged, although the count rises. Refining a partition into subvenues with different compositions weakly lowers W and raises B, with pooled composition fixed. That is the variance-decomposition consequence of refinement. It is not evidence that opening a new establishment changed anybody’s behaviour. Duplicate POIs, category changes and shifts in spatial resolution therefore require direct auditing.

Finite-count effects under an explicit toy model

An additional effect arises from estimating composition with finite samples. Consider a deliberately idealized no-sorting model: every venue draws independent multinomial observations from the same true group vector p; sample sizes are fixed positive integers n_j; N=sum_j n_j; and weights are n_j/N. Plug-in fractionalization then satisfies:

E[F(p_hat_j)] = (1 - 1/n_j) F(p)
E[P_hat] = (1 - 1/N) F(p)
E[W_hat] = (1 - J/N) F(p)
E[B_hat] = ((J-1)/N) F(p)

Thus at fixed total sample size, more venues can produce a larger estimated between component even when true venue compositions are identical. This is an analytic illustration, not a simulation or a correction fitted to project data. The expectation of B_hat/P_hat is not the ratio of these expectations. If sample size increases with venue count, the relationship changes; the displayed expression alone cannot explain the historical gradient.

Our measures use inferred home-area demographic shares and potentially expanded, suppressed and repeated observations. They are not independent categorical draws with known n_j. Applying the toy correction directly would be unjustified. A future sampling model must reflect the actual observation process, including repeated devices and shared origin-demographic estimates. The dedicated sampling-bias review owns the broader literature; this note supplies the algebraic implications without duplicating it.

Count also needs to be separated from concentration. Two markets with 100 listed venues can have very different effective numbers, 1/sum_j w_j^2. Rarely observed venues can inflate the nominal count and depress estimated within-venue diversity. A mean of market-level between shares is not the between share of pooled mean components. Changes in market weighting can therefore move the headline statistic without changing a representative visitor’s experience.

What the saved evidence can support

The saved 2019 count-bin table reports within-venue fractionalization rising from 0.2861 to 0.4162 and mean between shares from 1.11% to 8.34% between the 2-4 and 100-plus bins. These are historical selected-bin diagnostics, not a rerun, a causal relationship or evidence that the toy sampling model explains them. The reported correlations condition on subcategory, not the same local market. See the saved synthesis.

Those patterns are compatible with growing pooled diversity and a growing fraction allocated between venues. They do not imply that within-venue mixing falls with count. Residential composition, volume, geographic extent and establishment differentiation can all vary across bins. Treating the 933 MSA_CODE categories as 933 metropolitan areas would introduce a further interpretation error because micropolitan areas are included.

The available aggregate decomposition is useful for a measurement audit. It does not supply verified prices, product attributes, independent historical menus, individual choices or an establishment-entry design. Income group shares, rather than a scalar diversity score alone, are required to reproduce the decomposition on a consistent income definition. Origin-flow access is not authorized in this review.

Remaining contribution and decision rule

A nontrivial descriptive contribution would establish whether within-service sorting exceeds a credible mechanical benchmark and whether meaningful differentiation explains that excess. It should compare common residential support and opportunity definitions, not simply place a count coefficient beside controls. A causal entry paper would require a separately justified supply shock and evidence against simultaneous demand or neighbourhood change; neither follows from the existing panel.

The minimal outcome-blind exercise is to freeze market boundaries, category definitions, demographic normalization, weights and sample-size diagnostics. Verify identities and duplicate handling. Predefine equal-volume and common-support comparisons, alternative effective-count measures, and a synthetic no-sorting observation model matching the data-generating process as closely as possible. Specify how missing or suppressed observations enter before examining whether the count gradient survives. No such empirical exercise was executed here.

Advance only if a stable, economically meaningful excess remains and independent product or supply information can distinguish it from geography and demand. Otherwise retain the decomposition and count analysis as a measurement appendix. This recommendation lowers confidence in the current standalone count paper without asserting that its substantive mechanism is false. Main integration determines the final ranking.
