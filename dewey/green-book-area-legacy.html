Green Book: historical locations and present-day area legacies

20 October 2026. David's substantive clarification, recorded as a proposed research direction, not a new empirical result.

The question

Do neighbourhoods or commercial districts containing historical Green Book locations have different patterns of visitor-origin racial composition and mixing today, even when the original firms and buildings have disappeared?

The object is the area's legacy, not the survival of an establishment. Historical points can be assigned to a fixed contemporary geography, or to outcome-blind buffers or commercial centres. A demolished site still enters the historical denominator. No replacement POI inherits a Green Book label merely because it occupies the old address.

This makes building non-survival in note 103 a reason to avoid a surviving-building design, not a reason to reject the area-legacy question. The original notes and their dated findings remain available.

A useful first comparison

  1. Freeze a documented historical edition or listing window and a rights-cleared location frame, keeping unlocated and uncertain records. Distinguish unique historical sites from repeated listings; multiple annual entries for one site are not multiple institutions.
  2. Define contemporary areas without consulting mixing outcomes. Existing clusters are usable only if their construction is independent of racial composition and the outcome being explained. Otherwise use independently defined geography and predeclared spatial sensitivity checks.
  3. Map historical presence or site density into those areas, respecting coordinate uncertainty. Fix the primary historical exposure definition after the source audit but before inspecting modern outcomes.
  4. Compare current visitor-origin composition and mixing with comparable commercial areas in the same city. Retain areas with no observed modern POIs in coverage accounting; undefined diversity is missing, not zero. Report historical and modern sample selection separately.
  5. Distinguish three outcomes: estimated Black-origin visitor share, within-venue racial diversity, and separation across venues in an area. High Black participation does not necessarily mean high integration. These are area-based proxies, not observed individual race or interaction.

The baseline comparison should address historical residential composition, commercial activity and transport geography where those variables are actually observed. A second, explicitly conditional comparison can account for current neighbourhood composition. Current composition and redevelopment may be part of the legacy itself; controlling for them is not automatically a better estimate of the total association.

Mechanisms and limits

Persistence could operate through residential settlement, commercial networks, institutions, reputation or continuing patterns of avoidance. Redevelopment, transport change and migration could instead explain, remove or reverse the association. These are competing hypotheses, not established mechanisms.

Co-location of historical points and contemporary mixing cannot distinguish these explanations or identify the causal effect of historical inclusion. A claim about continuing avoidance would need origin-specific destination choices and credible accessible alternatives, beyond a map of area averages. Even that would not by itself identify discriminatory preferences. Historical listing is not evidence of integrated patronage, and nonlisting is not evidence of discrimination.

This remains a potential descriptive persistence contribution, subject to novelty and data feasibility. It does not become causal because the history predates the modern outcomes.

What is needed, and what is not

Needed for the area analysis: a versioned historical frame, listing dates, usable coordinates and uncertainty, permission for the intended linkage, an outcome-blind contemporary geography and comparison universe, and authorized modern location/composition inputs. County-centroid geocodes cannot support a precise local-buffer assignment.

Not a universal prerequisite: dated 2017-2019 operation or building survival for each historical business. Those histories are needed for firm-continuity claims, survivor subgroups or a specific redevelopment mechanism, not to define historical presence in an area.

Green Book area-legacy scope addendum

20 October 2026. Record of David's clarified target, before any modern linkage. This is a design-scope decision, not a frozen estimand, preregistration, data-access extension or empirical result.

Target and supersession

The main Green Book branch asks about contemporary neighbourhood or commercial- district composition around historical listed locations, irrespective of whether the original enterprise or building survives. Firm continuity is an optional separate branch, not the entry condition.

Do not overwrite the historical source-reconciliation protocols, receipts or reviewer workstreams. For the area branch only, replace any universal requirement for 2017-2019 operation/survival histories with verified historical listing dates, usable location evidence and fixed contemporary geography. Preserve rights, coverage, location-uncertainty and historical-unit checks. Detailed explanation and limitations are in note 107.

Before inspecting linked outcomes

  • Fix the edition/window and source version; audit complete coverage and reconcile repeated editions, businesses and physical sites.
  • Select areas from documentary and location quality, not modern survival, visitor demographics or an attractive mixing pattern.
  • Define geography independently of outcome composition. Do not define "high-mixing clusters" and then use the same outcome to validate them.
  • Choose one primary historical presence/density measure, geographic scale, comparison rule, weighting and small fixed sensitivity family. Set these after learning actual geocode precision, before linking to outcomes.
  • Separate listing presence from listing duration, which also reflects survival, directory coverage and reporting.
  • Keep unresolved historical sites and areas with absent modern measurement in denominator/attrition accounting, without fabricated zeros.
  • Specify Black-origin share, within-venue diversity and across-venue separation as distinct outcomes. Freeze what the authorized inputs can support and report the complete selected family.
  • Document inference units, spatial dependence, source coverage and disclosure rules before estimation. Venue counts do not create independent historical treatment observations when history varies at the district level.

Interpretation

The initial estimand is a descriptive historical-location association. Within-city and historical-geography controls reduce specific composition differences but do not make listing random. Contemporary residential composition, redevelopment and commerce may be mediators: present total descriptive associations separately from conditioned comparisons.

Continuing avoidance, institutional persistence and neighbourhood composition are competing explanations. No preference or discrimination mechanism is identified by area co-location alone. Do not label unlisted areas "discriminatory" or listed areas "integrated."

Status

No geographic scale, edition/window, causal instrument or modern match has been selected or estimated by this addendum. Existing data permissions are unchanged. Lack of a surviving original building is not a stop condition; unusable historical coordinates, an unsuitable frame or no defensible comparison can still require narrowing or stopping a local legacy contrast.
