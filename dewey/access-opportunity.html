Question and bottom line

Subsequent source check, 19 October: hash-matched IMLS Public Libraries Survey archives provide 17,267 outlet keys appearing in all three years, 2017-2019. A preliminary fixed-outlet/nonclosed/coordinate screen retains 16,812 candidate records in 2019. The new research report documents calendar, schema and interpretation limits. This is an independently sourced supply-list lead, not verified mobility coverage. No investment regression or failed historical-menu design was restarted.

Does unequal exposure reflect unequal available destinations, or different use of comparable opportunities? This is a stronger question than documenting a residential-experienced gap, but the distinction is not new. Its empirical content depends on measuring opportunities independently of realized choices. Conditioning on travel distance alone does not reveal an opportunity set, and a residual cannot be labelled a preference for segregation.

The most defensible current framing is an opportunity audit with explicit bounds and assumptions. A causal claim about transport or service provision would require an independently justified intervention design. The existing historical menu failures are central feasibility evidence, not a minor data-cleaning inconvenience.

It provides structural counterfactuals rather than an experimental access effect. Define an opportunity gap without overstating it

For origin o, let actual normalized destination shares be s_oj, benchmark opportunity weights be q_oj, and a fixed venue attribute be D_j. Then:

R_o = sum_j s_oj D_j
A_o = sum_j q_oj D_j
G_o = R_o - A_o

G_o describes allocation relative to a stated opportunity weighting. It does not uniquely decompose constraints and preferences. Uniform weights over establishments, weights by capacity, and travel-cost weights each embed different assumptions about what an opportunity means. An establishment can be physically near but unaffordable, closed during feasible hours, inaccessible by the relevant mode, or unsuitable for the service needed.

Moreover, demographic crowd composition is endogenous to everybody’s choices. Holding D_j fixed describes reallocation into existing crowds; it does not describe the equilibrium exposure after a large population reallocates. A genuine policy counterfactual must update crowd compositions, possibly congestion and supply, and state whose choices change. The fixed-composition diagnostic remains useful if labelled narrowly.

Opportunity weights must not be constructed solely from the same realized origin choices whose selectivity is being assessed. Choosing the menu as venues that the focal group visited guarantees that some exclusion is invisible. Using the union of all observed visitors’ venues improves support but still misses unobserved establishments and does not establish independent availability. Cross-fitting composition can reduce self-inclusion, but cannot manufacture missing menus.

Required inputs versus current evidence

The needed inputs are independently dated establishment existence and service classification; a defensible measure of capacity or weighting convention; historical travel feasibility; and approved origin-destination flows for actual use. Price, hours, disability access, employment locations and trip chaining could matter even if they are unavailable. Each omission narrows what the resulting residual means.

The project has saved selected-county grocery diagnostics and relative-distance comparisons. They are historical, selected observations, not a new nationally representative access result or a rerun in this review. Earlier attempts to construct independent historical menus failed coverage. Consequently there is presently no verified menu on which to base the proposed opportunity decomposition. See the saved synthesis.

Annual origin maps do not reveal travel mode, trip chains, household budgets, individual itineraries or the time at which people could shop. A route from a home-area centroid is not the route used during an actual trip. Current map availability cannot validate a historical route. Nor does a provider webpage establish a licence, a downloadable dataset or authorization to acquire it. This review obtained no datasets and inspected no restricted flows.

What could still be a contribution?

A credible remaining contribution would quantify how much can actually be concluded about unequal exposure when menus are incomplete, and then establish whether a substantive comparison survives defensible uncertainty. The target could be a specific service and a defined population for which opportunities genuinely differ, not a nationwide residual labelled access or tastes.

One useful design feature is an explicit missing-opportunity bound. If the missing share of opportunity weight is known to be m, the observed-menu average is A_obs, and the venue measure lies in [0,D_max], then:

(1-m)*A_obs <= A_true <= (1-m)*A_obs + m*D_max

This is an accounting bound, not a new estimator or a solution to unknown weights. Missing venue counts do not identify missing opportunity weight. For fractionalization, D_max=1-1/K with K exhaustive normalized groups; for an exposure share it is one. The bound is valid only when the observed and missing portions share the declared weighting convention. It can demonstrate that a purported access difference is unidentified even when most establishments are matched.

The substantive advance would come from a comparison that remains informative under these bounds and under plausible alternative service definitions, not from the bound itself. It would also need to say why existing studies cannot answer the particular economic question. A distinct country, index or year is not sufficient by itself.

Minimal feasibility and paper decision

Before inspecting outcome differences, select a bounded geography, service and historical period on measurement grounds. Inventory independent menu coverage, historical date validity, geocoding, travel modes and support for nonvisited alternatives. Fix acceptance thresholds and weighting rules before revealing group exposure. Retain a failure table so later attempts cannot quietly replace an unsuccessful historical source with a convenient contemporary proxy.

Only after that audit passes, and after separate authorization, should a pilot calculate observed-use and benchmark exposure on identical support, with missing-weight bounds and alternative cost rules. Predeclare whether the goal is descriptive access, a policy effect or structural choice; each requires different identifying assumptions. No regression or pilot was run here.

The recommendation is conditional continuation as a data-feasibility task, not promotion as an immediately executable paper. Relative to the bare directional idea, its substantive question is stronger; relative to current readiness, its independently verified data requirement is weaker. If historical independent opportunities remain unavailable, stop the access-versus-preference claim and use distance-adjusted descriptive exposure with explicit limits.
