# All-service income accounting: calculation complete, paired release withheld

20 October 2026. This follows the published full-2019 national accounting.
The question is whether within-county separation is widespread across
subcategories or mainly carried by a few services. The calculation is
completed; its proposed joint public release is not cleared.

## What ran

The new calculation covers every literal subcategory and both income lookups
on the same eligible venue universes as note 117. It reuses the verified
2019 venue vectors rather than repeating their reconstruction. For each
subcategory it decomposes total income separation into between-county and
within-county components, showing absolute normalized separation separately
from the within share. Full-Service Restaurants was the preselected example,
not a category selected after inspecting results.

The run took 105.4 seconds. Full-category weighted totals reproduce the
approved national accounting with maximum discrepancy `4.44e-16`. An
independent high-precision calculation on the fixed hash-selected sample
also passes, with maximum field discrepancy `4.44e-16`. Inputs and execution
fingerprints remain unchanged. There was no new source reconstruction,
original weekly processing, city-size regression or attitude regression.

## Why the table is not released

The proposed reporting format combines category results and top-five
concentration summaries for two lookups. Before running, the protocol required
checking not only the large displayed groups, but also small remainders that
their overlaps and differences could expose.

The large pooled groups and unpaired subgroup checks pass. A paired lookup
check fails: at least one subgroup generated by those overlapping selections
has a nonempty lookup-only remainder supported by fewer than 20 venues.
Under the fixed rule, the entire proposed joint release is withheld.
The specific label, exact count and private outcome values are not reported.
An independent reconstruction now confirms this stop, using a separate
full-outer-join implementation of lookup membership and recomputing the
support checks. The source and code hashes remain unchanged. Nineteen
artificial verifier tests passed before this separate 12.5-second real check.
This verifies the reason to withhold, not permission to release the table.

This is a conservative disclosure stop, **not a new null economic result
and not a failure to reproduce the national calculation**. No category
candidate file was produced, no restaurant result was substituted, and the
thresholds were not relaxed. The complete private calculation is preserved.
We therefore cannot yet report whether the residual is widespread across
services, its category percentiles or its concentration.

## What it changes

The separately approved national finding remains: 52.09% of preferred
between-venue income separation is within recorded county/subcategory,
compared with 54.32% using the lower-coverage strict lookup. Those are the
earlier national shares, not newly released category findings. They concern
home-neighbourhood income, not observed individual income or contact.

**Decision: narrow this reporting step, continue the research.** Preserve
the independently confirmed failed joint-release gate. A later coarser
release format would need an explicit separate rationale and review; it
must not be presented as the original planned release or chosen for favourable
economic findings. Proceed meanwhile with the already authorized geographic
question: how much separation remains within smaller, prospectively defined
areas? The coordinate file is fingerprinted and the public 2019 county
reference has been acquired and topology-checked, but no new geographic
linkage or smaller-area income estimate exists yet.

The all-category question is a descriptive qualification, not itself a
novel structural estimate. County and smaller-area comparisons do not prove
common choice sets, preferences or discrimination. Prior near-zero
distinct-venue results, correction 70a, negative equal-county validation,
expected-worker limitations and failed attitude families remain unchanged.
