Restaurant supply and city size: a public-data benchmark

20 October 2026. New empirical supporting analysis for rank 2, using public Census data only. Not the income-separation pilot, a causal mechanism test, or a claim of a new standalone contribution.

The result in plain English

Larger metropolitan areas have more restaurants, but full-service restaurant numbers rise almost in proportion to the number of residents. Across 308 geographically comparable metros, doubling population is associated with about 2.03 times as many full-service establishments, or 1.6% more per resident. The model-based interval includes exact proportionality.

This does not refute the proposed choice mechanism. Twice as many venues can still allow more sorting, even if population also doubles. A large positive relationship between city population and venue counts is therefore not sufficient evidence for that mechanism. We must measure accessible alternatives and use of those alternatives, not mistake the mechanical size relationship for evidence of segregation.

Data and model

The outcome is the published count of employer establishments in each of four fixed 2017 NAICS industries in the 2019 County Business Patterns metropolitan file. The regressor is log metropolitan population, constructed from Vintage 2019 county population. There is one observation per metro, one cross-section, equal metro weights, and an intercept. There are no individual incomes, visits, worker profiles, attitude outcomes, year effects or other controls in these regressions.

For each industry separately:

log⁡(Nm)=α+βlog⁡(Pm)+εm. \log(N_m)=\alpha+\beta\log(P_m)+\varepsilon_m.

Here NmN_m is establishments and PmP_m is residents. The reference value is beta = 1, which means proportionate supply, not beta = 0. Doubling population corresponds to a fitted count ratio of 2β2^\beta, and to 100[2β−1−1]100[2^{\beta-1}-1]% more establishments per resident. These are cross-sectional associations, not effects of making a city bigger.

Sources: 2019 CBP files, CBP county-membership reference, 2017 NAICS labels, and the previously verified population construction in note 99. The prospective protocol fixed the four industries and both geographic samples before establishment counts were downloaded or read.

Question and interpretation

Do larger metropolitan areas have proportionately more employer establishments within the same restaurant industry, or primarily more residents? An independent public count can establish a supply-side reference without relying on the mobility panel’s venue coverage. It does not identify accessible alternatives, product quality, cuisine, shared centres, income separation or preferences. This is supporting evidence for rank 2, not a standalone novelty claim.

Estimand

For each fixed industry k and metro m, estimate equally weighted OLS:

log(establishments_mk) = alpha_k + beta_k log(population_m) + error_mk.

Report n, beta, HC3 model-based standard error/95% interval, R-squared and population coverage. Translate beta into the fitted count ratio 2^beta and the percentage change in establishments per resident, 100*(2^(beta-1)-1), associated with doubling metro population. The proportional benchmark is beta=1, not beta=0. No causal language, significance stars or claim that these intervals capture Census measurement error, spatial dependence or sampling uncertainty in a randomly sampled metro population. The secondary estimates are descriptive, not four independent confirmatory tests.

Do not add post-result controls, outlier deletions, alternate years or category searches. Verify the primary least-squares coefficient and HC3 covariance with independent arithmetic and preserve weak/contrary results. Produce a labelled scatter/fit and complete coefficient/support table.

Data construction

  1. Read county population summary level 050 for US50+DC, retaining FIPS as fixed-width text. Require a unique five-digit county key and positive integer 2019 population.
  2. Parse CBP’s supplied county-membership file. Require the exact columns, valid keys, unique county membership and consistent metro names/types. The word MSA in a filename does not make every record metropolitan: 551 micros are excluded.
  3. For each CBP metro compare its entire county set and metro status to the September 2018 reference. Only exact matches enter primary eligibility. Sum 2019 county population only if every member county is available. Exact-match sums must equal the previously built reference population.
  4. Read only msa, naics and est from the ZIP’s single cbp19msa.txt member. Validate unique metro-industry keys and inclusion in the supplied geography reference. Every published count must parse as an integer of at least three. Select the four predeclared industry codes.
  5. Join the official industry labels one-to-one. The legacy label file uses Windows-1252 punctuation; do not drop or guess undecodable labels.
  6. Left-join each industry’s published counts to each eligible geographic sample, with validate="one_to_one". Report unmatched cells; never fill them with zero or derive withheld counts from aggregate margins.
  7. Separately estimate each industry’s log-count model with an intercept, equal metro weights and HC3 (use_t=False) normal 95% intervals. Report both geographic samples and all four industries. Transform the slope against proportionality (one), not against no association (zero).

Interpretation and limitations

The observed supply gradient is descriptive. Population and establishment counts are both measures of scale; a high R-squared is not mechanism identification. Full-service proportionality is compatible with the fixed model, not proven exactly. Unpublished small cells especially select the buffet sample. There is no model for that selection, no claim of spatially robust inference and no Census-uncertainty adjustment.

No count is an independently verified historical accessible choice set. Metropolitan totals omit within-metro distances, opening times, prices, products, capacity and individual constraints. The benchmark therefore neither validates nor rejects income sorting within a common menu.

Note 102 supplements the rank-2 proposal and note 99’s population reference. It does not supersede any original protocol or historical result, relax the named-source approval gate or restart a failed public-facing design.

Green Book: a Winston-Salem feasibility qualification

20 October 2026. Public-source finding for rank 3; not a historical sample reconstruction, modern POI match or economic estimate.
