The question

Do everyday places bring people from different social backgrounds together? The original project asks which activities create that exposure, whether public-facing work matters, and whether exposure is associated with attitudes or social connections.

The present data are strongest on the composition of recorded visits. They do not directly observe conversation, friendship, personal income or the employees at each establishment. A credible next paper therefore needs a clear descriptive contribution before making claims about interaction or attitudes.

Current recommendation: the measurement-led descriptive paper now has a complete coauthor-review rough draft, but its novelty case and inherited weekly-to-annual construction still need work. It is not submission-ready; Green Book and other alternatives remain separate.

Additional weighting audit: the annualization check completed its private calculation and reproduced earlier means, but failed the planned disclosure release. No new diversity means or weighting effects are published. The ranking is unchanged, and the original construction still needs independent replication.

What the expanded review changed

All eight individual reviews are complete. Each sets out the closest papers, the remaining contribution, required data, feasibility checks and reasons to stop. The source library records what was actually read. Some sources were inspected in full or in selected methods sections; others were accessible only as abstracts. This is a substantial focused review, not an exhaustive systematic review or proof of novelty.

  • Incoming versus outgoing is no longer the leading standalone idea. Renninger, O’Clery and Arcaute (2025) already compare local amenity segregation with residents’ isolation in a matched neighbourhood classification. A differently labelled map would not be enough.
  • Access benchmarks also have close predecessors. Comparing visitors with reachable populations is not itself new; Liao (2026) is particularly close. Our historical menu coverage remains a separate obstacle.
  • The venue-count pattern needs a measurement check before a behavioural explanation. More recorded venues can change estimated separation through partitioning and finite information per venue. This is a possible explanation to test, not a finding that explains our actual gradient. The completed distinct-venue comparison now qualifies the adjusted count gradient; it removes same-venue pair mass, not composition noise.
  • Green Book is the best separate alternative to screen for feasibility. The narrower question distinguishes persistence of a firm, its site and its surrounding district. Existing access and urban-renewal research means historical listing alone cannot identify a discrimination effect.

These literature conclusions supersede the earlier plan’s ranking and novelty discussion, not historical estimates. The subsequent bounded empirical authorization and completed checks are recorded above; broader inputs and failed inference gates remain restricted.

What we already know

These are selected saved project results, not new estimates from the literature review. The earlier evidence appendix supplies the result-specific context and source-note names.

Finding Interpretation
Within-venue racial diversity: 0.4358; pooled local-service diversity: 0.4811. The difference is 0.0453, or 9.41% of the pooled value. Combining visitors across establishments makes a service market look more mixed than the average visit within an establishment. This is accounting on retained support, not an attitude effect.
In the 2019 count pilot, within-venue diversity and between-venue separation both rise across venue-count bins. More recorded venues do not mean less absolute within-venue mixing. Differences in local population, volume, coverage and supply remain possible explanations.
The public-facing extension has not passed its inference checks. All four prospective industry-concentration screens failed. No corrected public-facing coefficient was estimated. Expected workers are not observed employees.
The downstream results are mixed. Some connectedness associations are positive, but validation is not uniform: the saved equal-county comparison was negative. Current county attitude tests did not survive multiple-testing adjustment; a separate aligned family analysis stopped for insufficient support before estimation.

Income describes home areas, not individual earnings. The racial measure infers composition from home areas and conditions on four retained groups. The earlier correction 70a concerns Atlas child weights, not Facebook-user weights; note 69’s local deviations are not category-fixed-effect coefficients. Those distinctions and the failed designs are retained in the evidence record.

Measurement and empirical approach

First distinguish three objects: the diversity inside a venue, the diversity experienced by an origin area’s residents across their visits, and the average diversity of venues located in a destination area. They answer different questions. Pooling everyone across separate establishments creates a fourth object and generally gives a different answer.

The measurement guide explains these objects with a small numerical example, then explains what a proposed regression would compare. Exact formulas and assumptions remain available in the individual technical reviews; they are not a substitute for a clear economic question.

Proposed action plan

  1. Validate the construction. Pin source versions and rules, approve an exact input sample, and independently compare complete-week annual flows before corrected indices. Release an aggregate mismatch and coverage matrix.
  2. Freeze one descriptive question. Choose the service comparison and interpretation to be tested before selecting attractive categories. Specify the benchmark, coverage rules, weights and a result that would count against the proposed explanation.
  3. Use the completed matched-sample comparison to narrow the count claim. Adjusted distinct-venue contrast is near zero despite positive original between-venue and separation-share associations. Observation intensity, duplicate handling and a defensible observation-process baseline still need their own checks; this comparison does not apply the illustrative multinomial correction.
  4. Decide whether a paper remains. Continue only if the resulting fact is economically important, robust enough to describe and clearly distinct from the closest work. Otherwise retain a measurement appendix, narrow the question or stop that route.
