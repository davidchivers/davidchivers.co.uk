Start with the distinction

A place can attract a mixed population without people mixing within the same establishment. Residents can also leave their neighbourhood and experience very different places. These are related questions, but they need different measures.

The project uses recorded origin-to-venue activity and census characteristics of visitors’ home areas. It does not observe the race or earnings of each visitor, who spoke to whom, or who works at a particular venue. The calculations below describe the recorded, demographically matched activity, not the entire population.

Four measures, four questions

Measure Plain-English calculation Question it answers
Diversity within a venue Take the estimated group shares among that venue’s patrons. The fractionalization index is one minus the sum of their squared shares. How mixed is this venue’s recorded patronage over the period?
Resident-based exposure Multiply the diversity of each destination by visits from the origin area. Add these products, then divide by all covered visits from that origin. How diverse are the venues that this area’s residents visit?
Destination-based exposure Average the diversity of venues located in an area, weighting each venue by its covered incoming visits. How diverse is the average recorded visit to a venue in this area?
Pooled local-service diversity Combine visitor group shares across all establishments in an area and service category, then calculate diversity. How mixed is the service sector’s combined patronage, even if groups use different establishments?

For normalized group shares, fractionalization is the probability that two independent draws from the inferred composition belong to different groups. It is not the observed probability of two actual people meeting. Its maximum depends on the number of groups: 0.5 for two equally sized groups, 0.75 for four, and 0.9 for ten.

The project has other indices, including entropy and related segregation measures. The within/between accounting results discussed here use fractionalization. Their exact formulas and numerical scales must not be transferred to other indices without checking.

A small example

These numbers are invented to explain the calculation; they are not project observations.

Following residents

Suppose residents of one neighbourhood make 80 covered visits to a venue with diversity 0.2 and 20 visits to a venue with diversity 0.5. Their resident exposure is:

(80 visits x 0.2 + 20 visits x 0.5) / 100 visits = 0.26.

The destination-based measure instead follows venues in the neighbourhood, including visitors who live elsewhere. It could therefore be very different locally. Across the whole system, however, the two flow-weighted averages must agree when they use exactly the same visits and venue scores. A national discrepancy would signal different definitions, support or weights, not a new social effect.

Following establishments

Now imagine two equally visited cafes. Each has patrons exclusively from one of two different groups. Within each cafe, diversity is zero. Pool the cafes and the combined population is half from each group, giving diversity 0.5. The difference is separation between cafes.

If both cafes instead have an equal mix of the two groups, within-cafe and pooled diversity are both 0.5. There is no between-cafe difference. Neither example establishes actual conversation; even a mixed annual visitor pool may use a venue at different times.

What the saved result means

The corrected audit reports four-group racial fractionalization of 0.4358 within venues, compared with 0.4811 after pooling visitors within area-subcategory-year cells. The gap is 0.0453, or 9.41% of pooled diversity. This is the portion of that accounting measure attributable to differences in establishment composition on the retained support.

It is not a 9.41% effect on attitudes, friendship or discrimination. It also does not mean that 9.41% of people are segregated. These are saved audit results, not a new regression.

Income and race stay separate

  • Income: the original groups are based on income characteristics of home census block groups. They are not personal earnings. Ten unweighted block-group income ranks are not ten equally sized groups of people.
  • Race: composition is inferred from home-area census shares. The displayed racial accounting conditions on four retained groups: non-Hispanic White, non-Hispanic Black, non-Hispanic Asian and Hispanic. Omitted group mass needs separate reporting.
  • Workers: expected workforce demographics describe an industry-area proxy, not the people observed working at each venue. A public-facing occupational score does not directly measure the duration or quality of interaction.

The final exports do not contain the ten income shares needed to repeat the same within/between decomposition for income. A scalar income-diversity index cannot reconstruct those missing shares.

What a regression would compare

The first step is to show distributions, coverage and within-versus-pooled comparisons. A subsequent proposed descriptive regression could compare venue diversity across service subcategories, allowing each geographic area to have its own baseline in each year. Accessible residential composition and independently measured venue characteristics could be added only when their definitions and coverage are defensible.

Element Proposed specification
Observation One venue in one year. Retain 2017, 2018 and 2019, with annual and common-support checks.
Dependent variable A named diversity index, with race and home-area income reported separately.
Main regressors Subcategory indicators, with a declared reference category; a top-category version can be shown separately.
Geographic adjustment Area-by-year fixed effects: comparisons within a defined area and year. First resolve the area’s geographic vintage and distinguish metro from micro areas.
Additional controls Predeclared opportunity and venue characteristics, only if measured independently and sufficiently well.
Weights Visit weights for the average recorded visit; equal-venue results for the average recorded establishment. Report both questions explicitly.
Uncertainty A prespecified dependence and clustering strategy appropriate to the regressor’s level. Millions of venue-years are not millions of independent shocks.

The subcategory coefficients would be conditional associations, not the causal effect of changing a venue’s type. Area-by-year effects absorb factors common to an area in that year; they do not remove neighbourhood-level access, endogenous venue locations, prices or visitor selection. Venue fixed effects would absorb a category that never changes within a venue.

This model has not been estimated in this review. The historical public-facing extension remains separate: its prospective industry-concentration checks failed and no corrected coefficient was produced. A proposed new model does not override those checks.

The next test should have a reason to fail

For venue variety, report separation share, absolute between-venue diversity, within-venue diversity and the distinct-venue contrast together: the completed same-sample comparison narrows the adjusted count claim without correcting noise or identifying preferences. Observation-intensity and common-support limits remain. For access, require an independent historical menu before interpreting unused opportunities. For incoming/outgoing, show both coordinates and service composition before naming a neighbourhood type.

More controls cannot repair an incorrectly defined outcome or missing alternatives. The quality review gives the remaining construction checks; the full technical reviews give formulas, assumptions, close predecessors and stop rules.
