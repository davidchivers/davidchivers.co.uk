  • Migrants gain D + F. The origin total D includes emigrants; those who remain lose F overall.
  • World output rises by C + D. The gains are uneven: some workers and capital owners lose.
  • Winners could compensate losers in principle, but compensation is not automatic.

All changes compare the post-migration equilibrium with the closed-economy allocation in the preceding graph, holding capital fixed. Existing destination residents: (B + C) - B = C. Original origin-country residents, including emigrants: (D + F) + I - (F + I) = D. Origin residents who stay: I - (F + I) = -F. Migrants are included once, in the origin population total. Summing the two original populations gives C + D, equal to the increase in world output. These are model income changes, not a claim that migration makes every individual better off.
