Budget sets.

£24 to spend. Coffee £3, lunch £6. Which baskets can you afford, and what moves when income or a price changes?

The affordability rule

coffee price × coffees + £6 × lunches ≤ budget

Coffee and lunch are treated as divisible, so 4.5 coffees is allowed. The controls move in halves only for convenience.

This is the starting line: £24, coffee £3, lunch £6. Every pound is spent: the basket sits exactly on the line.

The line shows what’s possible. Not what’s best.

In the starting example, every basket on the line spends exactly £24: 8 coffees, 4 lunches, or 4 of one and 2 of the other. Affordability alone can’t choose between them.

To find a best choice you also need preferences: how much this person values coffee against lunch. That is utility maximisation, the next lesson in the course.
