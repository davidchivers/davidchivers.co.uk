TOPIC 01 / INDIVIDUAL CHOICESWorked example · No calculus

The Economics Studio

A budget.
A world of choices.

What can you afford—and what changes when a price moves? Make a prediction, move a few things around, and see the economics for yourself.

01

The idea in one sentence
Your budget set contains every combination you can afford; its boundary is the budget line.

Learn by changing something

Build your own basket.

Illustrative model

You have £24 to spend on coffees at £3 each and lunches at £6 each. We treat quantities as divisible and hold the lunch price fixed. Can you find different ways to spend the same budget?

WHAT TO NOTICE

The line joins 8 coffees to 4 lunches. Along it, one extra coffee costs half a lunch. A point below the line leaves some money unspent.

Affordability is only the beginning. This graph does not tell us which bundle you prefer. Preferences—and then utility maximisation—come next. Being on the line does not by itself make a bundle optimal.

Pause. Predict. Explain.
