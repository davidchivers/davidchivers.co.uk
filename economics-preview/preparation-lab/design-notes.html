My recommendation

A course companion, built one topic at a time.

Use Quarto as the editable home for explanations, worked examples and printable material. Pair selected lessons with small HTML activities. Keep the original videos and assessments in place while testing whether students find the companions useful.

  1. Start with the budget constraint. This sample has a concrete learning objective and a direct comparison with the existing lesson.
  2. Agree the teaching content with Seva. The new example is illustrative, not a verified transcript. Decide where it belongs and how it should be linked.
  3. Try it with a few incoming students. Ask them to explain an income shift and a price pivot, then use their difficulties to revise the activity.

Where interaction could help next

Preferences and utility: choose, then explain

Let students compare bundles and trace an indifference curve under a clearly stated utility function. Keep preference assumptions explicit and distinguish utility from expenditure. This would connect directly to Topic 1.

Risk: compare a certain amount with a lottery

Change probabilities and payoffs, compare expected value with expected utility, and ask why the rankings can differ. Use a specified utility function and a valid wealth domain; the activity should teach the distinction rather than score risk attitudes.

Growth: move a saving rate and follow the adjustment

A Solow diagram could show how a different saving rate changes the steady-state level and transition. Keep the model assumptions visible, and avoid suggesting that a higher saving rate creates permanent per-capita growth in the basic model without technological progress.

Mathematics: short, optional checks where they are useful

Offer a worked algebra or calculus refresher beside the lesson that needs it. Establish the programme’s actual prerequisites before adding any diagnostic or recommending a new preparation sequence.

Scope and checks

The original course outline and the budget-constraint page were inspected; the full video library was not audited. The new example and its calculations are separate teaching proposals. No course files or student records were changed. The sample does not save progress or submit answers.

Quarto supports HTML documents and several forms of interactivity; normal rendering alone does not turn a lesson into a student-editable application. The studio here uses small, ordinary browser scripts and needs no server-side computation.
